Rubik's Cube Solving Robot
Personal Project (Jun 2026 - Present)

This project is an automated Rubik's Cube solver that combines mechanical design, embedded systems, and electronics. So far, I have designed a custom cube rotation mechanism in SolidWorks and manufactured the prototype using 3D printing. I also developed embedded C++ firmware on an ESP32 to control a NEMA 17 stepper motor through a TMC2209 driver, allowing the mechanism to rotate the cube with precise angular positioning. This project is still in development, with future work including a custom PCB, autonomous cube-solving functionality, and better mechanical design for improved stability

64-bit RISC-V Execution Unit
Digital Systems Design - ENSC 350, SFU (Apr - May 2026)

In this project, I designed and verified a modular 64-bit RISC-V execution unit in VHDL that implemented arithmetic, logical, comparison, branch, and configurable barrel shifter operations for FPGA implementation. I developed and compared several adder architectures, including Ripple Carry, Carry-Select, and Kogge-Stone designs, to evaluate hardware resource utilization and timing performance on Cyclone IV and Arria II FPGAs. I also created a comprehensive ModelSim testbench with automated test vectors to verify functionality and synthesized each design in Intel Quartus Prime to analyze RTL structures, FPGA resource usage, and worst-case propagation delays. The image shows the baseline Ripple Carry Adder used as the foundation of the execution unit.

Folded-Cascode CMOS Op-Amp Design
Microelectronics II - ENSC 325, SFU (Mar - Apr 2026)

This project involved designing and simulating a folded-cascode CMOS operational amplifier using OrCAD X PSpice. I iteratively optimized transistor sizing, biasing, and frequency compensation to meet challenging performance targets while balancing gain, bandwidth, stability, and power consumption. The final design achieved 74 dB open-loop gain, 1.73 MHz bandwidth, a 67° phase margin, a 1.33 V/μs slew rate, and 0.42 mW power consumption, giving me hands-on experience with analog CMOS circuit design and performance optimization. The image shows the final folded-cascode op-amp schematic with the biasing and compensation networks.

Spine-Align: Posture Monitor
Embedded and Real Time System Software - ENSC 351, SFU (Sept - Dec 2025)

Spine-Align is a wearable posture-monitoring device built on the BeagleY-AI platform. It uses an analog accelerometer attached to the user's back to read through the MCP3208 ADC to measure upper-back tilt and classify posture in real time. The system provides instant feedback using an LED indicator and allows users to adjust posture thresholds and sensitivity through an onboard joystick. The image shows the final prototype on a test subject, with the accelerometer mounted on the upper back and the BeagleY-AI board on the chest.

Cascaded Notch & Butterworth Filter
Electric Circuits II - ENSC 320, SFU (Aug 2025)

This project focused on designing an analog filter capable of removing both 120 Hz mains interference and high-frequency noise from biomedical signals. I modeled and optimized the filter in MATLAB and LTspice before building and testing it on a breadboard. Through frequency-response measurements and laboratory testing, I confirmed that the final design met the required performance targets while closely matching the theoretical and simulated results. The figure shows the final cascaded filter circuit, consisting of a 120 Hz notch filter (left) followed by a low-pass Butterworth filter (right).
